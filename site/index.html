Why Petti

A shoebox of slips nobody can match to anything. Approvals buried in a WhatsApp chat. A cash count that is N$200 short, and no one knows why.

Petti replaces all three. Every request has a reason, an approver, a receipt and a name against it, so the box always balances and the auditor finds everything in one file.

From “I need cash” to a closed month, in four steps

  1. 1Ask

    Anyone on the team asks for cash, claims back what they paid themselves, or reports that the office is out of something. It takes under a minute on their phone.

  2. 2Approve

    Small amounts go straight to finance. Anything over your limit goes to the GM or MD first, with what it will leave in the box shown before they say yes.

  3. 3Pay out and prove

    Finance hands over the cash and decides what proof must come back. The receipt is scanned on the phone, read automatically, and the change is recorded.

  4. 4Count and close

    Count the box and Petti shows the difference straight away. At month end one tap builds the pack for your accountant, with every voucher and receipt inside.

The controls your auditor asks for

Petti was built with an authority schedule in mind: who may approve what, and proof that they did. Nothing is approved by the person who asked for it.

  • Approval limits by person and amount. Above your threshold it goes to the GM, above theirs to the MD.
  • A name and a time on every step. Who asked, who approved, who paid, who checked.
  • Receipts that read themselves. Blurred or cut-off photos are refused with the reason, so every scan is usable.
  • Spending without a receipt, listed. A taxi or a street vendor is allowed, but it is named, explained and shown to management.
  • Cash counts signed off by the GM, with any difference explained.
  • The month-end pack in one tap: reconciliation, vouchers and scanned receipts in a single PDF, plus an export for your accounting package.

And the office stock, too

Toner, paper, coffee, cleaning stock: Petti knows what is on the shelf, how fast it goes and when it will run out.

  • Staff report “running low” in two taps instead of walking to the store.

  • Deliveries are checked in against what was ordered.
  • The monthly restock order fills itself in from real use.
